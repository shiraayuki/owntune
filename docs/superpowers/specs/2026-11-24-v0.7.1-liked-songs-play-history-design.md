# v0.7.1 — Liked Songs and play history (design)

## Intent

The second half of M4 is the Home tab with two sub-tabs, **For You** and **Stats** (mockups in `docs/mockups/home-v1/`), plus **Liked Songs**. It ships in three releases:

1. **v0.7.1 (this document):** Liked Songs, and the data that Home needs, recorded silently: a play history and where each play came from.
2. **v0.7.2:** For You.
3. **v0.7.3:** Stats.

Why the history comes first: today the database keeps only `play_count` and `last_played_at` per track, and a play counts only when a song runs to its end. Stats needs listening time per day, a streak, time of day, top lists per week/month/year. For You needs "recently played playlists/albums" and "Continue listening". None of that can be reconstructed later, so the history has to start recording as early as possible, before any of the Home UI exists.

Liked Songs follows Spotify (the user's reference for player interactions): a heart in Now Playing, a pinned "Liked Songs" collection, newest liked first.

## Goals

- **Like a song** from Now Playing (a heart to the right of title/artist), from the song menu ("Add to Liked Songs" / "Remove from Liked Songs", also in the menu opened from the player), and from the Add to Playlist sheet (so the multi-select bar and the album/artist "…" can like many songs at once).
- **Liked Songs collection**: a fixed row in the Playlists tab and a page like a playlist page (Play, Shuffle, songs newest liked first; tapping a song plays the collection from it).
- **One play rule everywhere:** a play counts once 30 seconds of it have been heard (Spotify's rule), or when a song shorter than 30 seconds plays to its end. This replaces "counts when the song ends" for `play_count` and `last_played_at`. Existing counts stay as they are; only new plays follow the new rule.
- **Play history:** one row per listen — which track, when it started, how much was actually heard (pauses and seeks excluded), whether it counted as a play, and where it was played from.
- **Queue source:** the queue knows what it was started from (album, artist, playlist, Liked Songs, Songs, Search), not just a label. Saved with the player state so it survives a restart.

## Non-goals

- Any Home UI (For You and Stats are v0.7.2 / v0.7.3). Nothing in this release *shows* the history.
- A heart on the mini player, the lock screen / notification, or song rows; a "liked" indicator in lists.
- Unlike in bulk (multi-select only adds to Liked Songs; removing is per song).
- Sorting or reordering Liked Songs, Edit/Delete on it.
- Reading or writing likes/ratings from/into file tags (POPM, MP4 rate).
- Backfilling the history from existing `play_count` values.
- Showing a listen history or "recently played" list anywhere yet.

## Anti-goals

- Losing a like through Edit Info, a cover change or a re-import attempt of the same file.
- History recording that slows down, blocks or breaks playback. History writes are fire-and-forget on the playback path; a failed write is dropped silently (like `markPlayed` today), never a dialog, never a stopped song.
- Counting one listen twice (e.g. once at 30 s and again at the end), or counting seeks as listening time.
- A database migration that loses existing users' tracks, playlists or settings.

## Constraints

- Everything from CLAUDE.md: logic testable on Linux without Rust (`FakeAudioEngine`, provider overrides via `pumpApp`), English everywhere, `material_ui` / `cupertino_ui` imports, errors visible in the UI, generated drift code committed.
- Foreign keys stay off; anything that references tracks is cleaned up explicitly (or deliberately kept, see below).
- iOS may end the app in the background without warning. The history must not depend on a clean shutdown.

## Approaches

**Likes**
1. **A nullable `liked_at` column on `tracks`** *(chosen)*. A like belongs to the song: Edit Info keeps it, deleting the song removes it, "newest liked first" is a sort. It flows through `tracksProvider` to every screen with no new provider plumbing.
2. Liked Songs as a built-in row in `playlists`. Reuses the playlist page, but needs exceptions everywhere: playlists allow duplicates, Liked Songs must not; Edit/Delete must be hidden; the duplicate prompt behaves differently. More special cases than it saves.

**History**
1. **A `plays` table, one row per listen** *(chosen)*, updated while the listen goes on, at the moments the player already saves its state. Every Stats figure (per day/week/month/year, time of day, streak, top lists, recap) and For You's "recently played" can be computed from it.
2. Daily counters only (listening time per day). Smaller, but cannot answer top songs per period, time of day or recently played sources. Rejected.

**Source**
- The queue's `String? source` label becomes a `QueueSource` (kind + key + label). There is no sensible alternative: For You must be able to reopen "Roadtrip" or an album, and a label alone is ambiguous (two albums can share a title; a playlist can be renamed).

## Design

### Database (schema v4)

Migration `from < 4`:
- `tracks.liked_at` — `DateTime`, nullable. Null = not liked.
- New table `plays`:
  - `id` integer primary key
  - `track_id` integer (references `tracks`, no FK enforcement)
  - `started_at` DateTime — when the listen started
  - `listened_ms` integer — time actually heard
  - `counted` boolean — whether it counted as a play
  - `source_kind` text, nullable — `QueueSourceKind.name`
  - `source_key` text, nullable — the source's key (see below)
- Index on `plays.started_at` (every Stats query is a time range).

`test/data/migration_test.dart` gets a v3 → v4 case: tracks, playlists and settings survive, `liked_at` is null, `plays` is empty.

**Deleting a track keeps its `plays` rows** *(orchestrator's call)*: the history is what was listened to, and listening time for a past week should not shrink because a song was deleted later. Stats/For You will simply skip rows whose track no longer exists when they need track details. `liked_at` goes with the track row.

### Likes

- `TrackRepository.setLiked(Iterable<int> ids, {required bool liked})`: liking sets `liked_at = now` only on tracks that are not liked yet (an existing like keeps its date, so re-liking from a bulk add does not reorder); unliking sets it to null.
- **Now Playing:** a heart button on the right of the title/artist row (`CupertinoIcons.heart` / `heart_fill`), filled in the accent colour when liked (in light mode the theme's accent token, whatever Paper uses). Only in the cover view; the compact row above Up Next does not get one.
- **Song menu:** "Add to Liked Songs" / "Remove from Liked Songs" with a heart icon, placed next to "Add to Playlist…". Present wherever the song menu appears, including the one opened from the player.
- **Add to Playlist sheet:** a "Liked Songs" row (heart tile) as the first target, below "New Playlist…". Adding likes the songs that are not liked yet; already-liked songs are skipped without the "Already added" prompt (Liked Songs has no duplicates). Then the sheet closes with the same feedback as adding to a playlist. Multi-select ends afterwards, as after Add to Playlist.
- **Playlists tab:** a "Liked Songs" row directly under "New Playlist", above the playlists, always shown (also with 0 songs and with no playlists): heart tile in the accent colour (like the "liked" tile in `docs/mockups/home-v1/01-for-you.html`), title "Liked Songs", subtitle "N songs · duration" (`formatTotalDuration`). The tab's search filters it by its name like a playlist. The "Create a playlist…" empty hint still shows when there are no playlists.
- **Liked Songs page:** same layout as the playlist page — heart tile as the big cover, "Liked Songs", "N songs · duration", Play and Shuffle, the songs sorted by `liked_at` descending. No "…" in the title row. Tapping a song plays the list from that song with source `liked`. Unliking a song (from its menu or from Now Playing) removes it from the page right away. Empty state: "Songs you like appear here. Tap ♥ in the player to add one."

### Queue source

```dart
enum QueueSourceKind { songs, album, artist, playlist, liked, search, queued, other }

class QueueSource {
  final QueueSourceKind kind;
  final String? key;   // album: "<artistKey>\u0000<titleKey>" from AlbumKey; artist: artist key; playlist: id; else null
  final String label;  // what the player shows today ("Songs", album title, playlist name, "Liked Songs", "Search")
}
```

- `PlayQueue.source` and `PlayerController.playList(..., source:)` take a `QueueSource` instead of a `String`. Every call site (Songs, Search, album, artist, playlist, Liked Songs pages; Play/Shuffle buttons) passes its kind and key.
- `PlayerSnapshot` saves `source` (label, as today), plus `sourceKind` and `sourceKey`. An old snapshot with only a label restores as kind `other` with that label; an unknown kind is treated as `other`. A broken source never invalidates the rest of the snapshot.

### Listen sessions and the 30-second rule

All in `PlayerController`, with the arithmetic in a small pure class (e.g. `ListenSession`) so it can be unit-tested without the controller.

- **A session starts** when an entry starts loading (`_start`) and **ends** when another entry starts, the player stops (`_stop`), or the current entry completes. Repeat one starts a new session on every round. Restoring the saved player after a restart starts a new session when playback resumes.
- **A long pause ends the session** *(orchestrator's call)*: when playback resumes after a pause of 30 minutes or more, the old session is closed and a new one starts (new `started_at`, listened time and the 30 s count start from zero). Otherwise a song paused at night and resumed in the morning would put all its time on the previous evening, which would skew the per-day, time-of-day and streak figures in Stats. The pause length is measured with wall-clock time, injected so tests can control it.
- **Reloading the current song keeps its session** *(orchestrator's call)*: `reloadIfCurrent` (after Edit Info or a cover change rewrote the file) only resets the position baseline; it does not end the session, reset the 30 s progress or count again.
- **Listened time** grows only while playing, from the engine's position updates: each forward step between two consecutive updates is added. The baseline resets on load and on every `seek` (in-app scrubber, system controls, "previous" restarting the song), so a seek never adds time. A step larger than a sanity cap (the builder picks it, a few seconds) is ignored.
- **Counted:** the moment listened time reaches 30 s — or the entry completes after at least 1 s was heard and either the track is shorter than 30 s or all of it but the last 3 s was heard (a seek to the end counts nothing) — the session is marked counted, and at that moment `play_count + 1` and `last_played_at = now` are written (replacing today's `markPlayed` call on completion). Once per session.
- **Writing the row:** the session's row is inserted at its first save point with `listened_ms ≥ 1000` and updated at later save points: the existing ones (every 5 s of playback, on pause, on queue changes) and when the session ends. A session that never reaches 1 s writes no row. All writes go through a `PlayRepository` (or `TrackRepository` methods) and are not awaited by playback; errors are swallowed.
- `started_at` is the wall-clock time the session started; `source_kind` / `source_key` come from the queue's `QueueSource` at that time — **except for entries added with Play Next / Play Later** *(orchestrator's call)*: those are recorded with kind `queued` and no key, so a single song queued into "Roadtrip" does not count as a Roadtrip play in For You's "recently played". This needs one flag per `QueueEntry` ("added by the user"), set by `insertNext` / `append`, kept through shuffle, `retain` and reordering, and saved in `PlayerSnapshot` (as the list of flagged positions; missing in old snapshots = none flagged). `QueueSourceKind` gains `queued` for this. Play Next / Later into an empty queue starts a queue whose source is `other`, and its entries are flagged as well.
- An abrupt end (iOS kills the app) loses at most the last few seconds of `listened_ms`; the row and the count already written stay.

### Testing

- Unit: `ListenSession` (steps, seeks, cap, 30 s threshold, short songs, once-only counting); `QueueSource` and `PlayerSnapshot` round trip incl. old snapshots; `TrackRepository.setLiked` (keeps existing date, unlike); migration v3 → v4.
- Controller tests with `FakeAudioEngine`: a 40 s listen counts once and writes one row; a pause of 30 min or more starts a new row on resume, a shorter one does not; a reload of the current song keeps the session; a Play Next entry is recorded as `queued`; a skip at 10 s writes a row with `counted = false` and no play count; a seek forward does not add time; repeat one creates one row per round; completion no longer double counts.
- Widget: heart in Now Playing toggles; song menu entry text follows the state; Liked Songs row in Playlists tab (count, search); Liked Songs page order, play from a song, empty state; Add to Playlist → Liked Songs skips already-liked songs.
- Integration (`integration_test/`): like/unlike against the real database; a play row is written during real playback on Linux.

- The sanity cap for a position step, and whether to use the position stream or an injected clock, as long as seeks never count and it is testable with `FakeAudioEngine`.
- Exact heart-button size/padding in Now Playing, matching the existing controls.
